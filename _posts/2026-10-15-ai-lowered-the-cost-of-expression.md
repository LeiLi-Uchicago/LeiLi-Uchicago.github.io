Several friends and colleagues have asked why I've been posting so often lately.

The honest answer: my account has not been hacked, and I didn't suddenly start thinking. 𝗔𝗜 𝗷𝘂𝘀𝘁 𝗺𝗮𝗱𝗲 𝗶𝘁 𝗺𝘂𝗰𝗵 𝗰𝗵𝗲𝗮𝗽𝗲𝗿 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝗺𝗲 𝘁𝗼 𝘀𝗮𝘆 𝘄𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗜 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗸.

As a non-native English speaker, writing a polished post on a professional platform used to take me hours. Most ideas never made it out of my head, because I decided to save my brain and hair for paper and proposal writing.

But with AI, my process now looks like this:

1. I start with 𝗼𝗻𝗲 𝗰𝗼𝗿𝗲 𝗶𝗱𝗲𝗮 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗮 𝗿𝗼𝘂𝗴𝗵 𝗱𝗿𝗮𝗳𝘁, sometimes even in Chinese.
2. An LLM helps me 𝘀𝘁𝗿𝘂𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲 𝗶𝘁 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝘀𝗺𝗼𝗼𝘁𝗵 𝗼𝘂𝘁 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗹𝗮𝗻𝗴𝘂𝗮𝗴𝗲.
3. I go back and forth a few rounds, 𝗱𝗲𝗹𝗲𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗮𝗻𝘆𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗱𝗼𝗲𝘀𝗻'𝘁 𝘀𝗼𝘂𝗻𝗱 𝗹𝗶𝗸𝗲 𝗺𝗲, and sometimes words I don't even recognize. (I'm serious. I had to look up what "delve" means.)
4. 𝗜 𝗰𝗵𝗲𝗰𝗸 𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘆 𝗰𝗹𝗮𝗶𝗺. Sometimes the model makes my point sound more certain than I am, or adds arguments I never made. Those get cut.

Some people feel AI-assisted posts are less authentic. I see it differently. 𝗔𝗜 𝗹𝗼𝘄𝗲𝗿𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗰𝗼𝘀𝘁 𝗼𝗳 𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻, 𝗻𝗼𝘁 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗰𝗼𝘀𝘁 𝗼𝗳 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴. The ideas, the judgment, and the responsibility for what gets published are still mine.

And step 4 is the same thing I keep writing about: 𝗔𝗜 𝗼𝘂𝘁𝗽𝘂𝘁 𝗻𝗲𝗲𝗱𝘀 𝘃𝗲𝗿𝗶𝗳𝗶𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻, whether it's a single-cell analysis or a LinkedIn post.

For scientists who think clearly but write slowly in a second language, this is a real shift. 𝗠𝗮𝗻𝘆 𝘃𝗼𝗶𝗰𝗲𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝘂𝘀𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗼 𝘀𝘁𝗮𝘆 𝗾𝘂𝗶𝗲𝘁 𝗰𝗮𝗻 𝗻𝗼𝘄 𝗯𝗲 𝗵𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗱.

Do you use AI in your writing? Where do you draw the line?

P.S. The downside of AI-assisted writing: if you meet me in person, you might wonder how the LinkedIn Shakespeare turned into the everyday regular normal guy next door. Same ideas, just fewer fancy words.
